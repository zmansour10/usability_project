2. Definitions and Standards
=========================================
Basic Terms and Expressions:
- Usability: "Extent to which a system, product or service can be used by specified users to achieve specified goals with effectiveness, efficiency and satisfaction in a specified context of use." (ISO 9241-11)
- Humans have remarkable perceptual and cognitive capabilities, which are usually heavily underestimated. Effective systems should take into account limitations of human perception, context of use, and goals/activities to be performed.

User-Centered Design (UCD):
- "A design philosophy and a process in which the needs, wants, and limitations of end users of a product are given extensive attention at each stage of the design process." [Wikipedia]
- "Human-centered design is an approach to interactive systems development that aims to make systems usable and useful by focusing on the users, their needs and requirements..." [ISO 9241-210]

[Image Description: A circular diagram showing the iterative User-Centered Design process. The 'User' is at the center. An outer loop shows the sequential phases: Research -> Concept -> Design -> Develop -> Release. Between the user and the outer loop are iterative 'Test / Analysis' cycles pointing back and forth, indicating continuous evaluation throughout the development cycle.]

The 6 Principles of User-Centered Design (ISO 9241-210):
1. Design is based upon an explicit understanding of users, tasks, and environments.
2. Users are involved throughout design and development.
3. Design is driven and refined by user-centered evaluation.
4. Iterative design process.
5. Design addresses the whole user experience.
6. Design team includes multidisciplinary skills and perspectives.

Benefits of a human-centered approach:
- Systems are easier to understand and use.
- Reduced discomfort, stress, and errors.
- Improved productivity of users and efficiency of organizations.
- Improved product quality, aesthetics, and impact (competitive advantage).

User Experience (UX):
- "A person's perceptions and responses resulting from the use and/or anticipated use of a product, system or service." (ISO 9241-210:2020)
- UX is an effect of brand image, design, functionality, performance, interactive behavior, helpfulness of the system, and environment.

Usability vs. User Experience:
- Before use: Anticipation (UX)
- While using: Effectiveness, Efficiency (Usability) + UX elements
- After use: Satisfaction (Usability), Fulfilment of expectation (UX)

Interactive System & Interface:
- Interactive System: Combination of hardware, software, and service components. Includes packaging, branding, user documentation, online help, support, and training.
- Interface: All components of an interactive system that provide information and controls necessary for the user to perform tasks.

=========================================
3. ISO 9241-11: Usability Definitions
=========================================
Gebrauchstauglichkeit (Usability): Ausmaß, in dem ein System durch bestimmte Benutzer in einem bestimmten Nutzungskontext genutzt werden kann, um Ziele effektiv, effizient und zufriedenstellend zu erreichen.
- Effektivität (Effectiveness): Genauigkeit und Vollständigkeit, mit denen Benutzer bestimmte Ziele erreichen.
- Effizienz (Efficiency): Verwendete Ressourcen in Bezug auf die erzielten Ergebnisse.
- Zufriedenstellung (Satisfaction): Ausmaß der Übereinstimmung der physischen, kognitiven und emotionalen Reaktionen des Benutzers mit Erfordernissen/Erwartungen.

Nutzungskontext (Context of Use):
Kombination von Benutzern, Zielen, Aufgaben, Ressourcen und Umgebung.

4. Nielsen: Usability 101
=========================================
What is Usability?
Defined by 5 quality components:
1. Learnability: How easy to accomplish basic tasks the first time?
2. Efficiency: How quickly can users perform tasks once learned?
3. Memorability: How easily can users reestablish proficiency after a period of not using it?
4. Errors: How many errors, how severe, and how easily can users recover?
5. Satisfaction: How pleasant is it to use the design?

Utility + Usability = Useful
- Utility: Does it provide the features you need?
- Usability: How easy & pleasant are these features to use?

Why is usability important?
- On the Web, usability is a necessary condition for survival. If it's hard to use, people leave.
- Best Practice: Spending about 10% of a design project budget on usability doubles a website's desired quality.

How to improve usability:
- User Testing with Representative users, Representative tasks, and Observation.
- Important: Users are tested individually and solve problems on their own. Helping them contaminates results.
- "You are not the user." "The user is always right (consistent problems are the system's fault)."
- Test 5 users (typically enough). Small iterative tests are better than one big study. Focus groups != User testing.

When to work on usability: At each stage of the design process. Start early and test every step.
Where to test: Anywhere quiet (doors closed). A notepad is the only needed equipment.

6. Heuristics & Rules of Thumb
=========================================
Nielsen's 10 Usability Heuristics (1994):
1. Visibility: Always show system status clearly & quickly.
2. Match with Real World: Use users' language & familiar concepts.
3. User Control & Freedom: Provide undo, redo, cancel.
4. Consistency & Standards: Follow conventions & stay consistent.
5. Error Prevention: Avoid problems before they happen.
6. Recognition over Recall: Make information visible.
7. Flexibility & Efficiency: Support shortcuts & customization.
8. Minimalist Design: Show only what's necessary.
9. Error Recovery: Clear messages + solutions.
10. Help & Documentation: Easy to find, concise, task-focused.

Shneiderman's 8 Golden Rules (1986):
1. Consistency: Use uniform layouts, terminology, and visual styles.
2. Universal Usability: Design for diverse users (e.g., dark mode, accessibility, shortcuts).
3. Informative Feedback: Provide immediate, clear system responses (e.g., "Message sent").
4. Completion-Oriented Dialogs: Indicate when tasks are completed.
5. Simple Error Handling: Show clear error messages with solutions.
6. Reversibility of actions: Allow users to undo actions.
7. Internal locus of control: Keep users in charge of actions.
8. Memory relief: Make information visible (e.g., recent items, active filters).

7. Digital Accessibility & Legal Requirements
=========================================
Barrierefreiheit als Marktzugangsvoraussetzung:
- The Curb-Cut-Effect: Barrierefreie Systeme steigern die Usability für alle Nutzer (z.B. Untertitel in lauten Umgebungen, hoher Kontrast bei Sonne).
- Inklusion ist Pflicht: Kein Add-on, sondern Menschenrecht (UN-BRK) und rechtliche Grundvoraussetzung.

[Image Description: A side-by-side illustration comparing accessibility features. On the left, a steep concrete staircase blocks a wheelchair user, a parent with a stroller, an elderly person with a cane, and a traveler with heavy luggage. A red "No Access" sign stands next to them, depicting frustration. On the right, a wide, curved ramp allows everyone—a wheelchair user, a person walking, a cyclist, and a child on a scooter—to travel easily and happily. This demonstrates the "Curb-Cut Effect" where accessibility benefits everyone.]

WCAG 2.2 Prinzipien (POUR):
1. Perceivable (Wahrnehmbar): Inhalte müssen mit den Sinnen erfassbar sein. (Check: Textalternativen vorhanden?)
2. Operable (Bedienbar): Navigation darf keine unmögliche Interaktion erfordern. (Check: Per Tastatur bedienbar? Genug Zeit?)
3. Understandable (Verständlich): Bedienung und Informationen klar & vorhersehbar. (Check: Einfache Sprache? Hilfreiche Fehler?)
4. Robust (Robust): Kompatibel mit aktuellen & zukünftigen Technologien (z.B. Screenreader). (Check: Code standardkonform?)

Gesetzlicher Rahmen & Standards:
- EU-Ebene: EU-Richtlinie 2016/2102 (öffentliche Stellen) & European Accessibility Act (EAA) 2019/882.
- Deutschland: BITV 2.0 (öffentliche Stellen des Bundes) & BFSG (Barrierefreiheitsstärkungsgesetz für Produkte/Dienste).
- EN 301 549: Harmonisierter europäischer Standard für Barrierefreiheit von IKT. Wer dies einhält, gilt rechtlich als konform.

Usability vs. Barrierefreiheit:
- Usability: Fokus auf Effektivität, Effizienz und Zufriedenheit in einem bestimmten Nutzungskontext.
- Accessibility (Barrierefreiheit): Fokus auf Zugänglichkeit und Nutzbarkeit für jeden Nutzer in jedem Kontext.
- Fazit: Gute Usability beginnt mit Barrierefreiheit. Ohne Barrierefreiheit gibt es keine gute Benutzerfreundlichkeit.
